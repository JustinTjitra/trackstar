# Ten-minute tests decide both DJ builds

Both projects are buildable from public, permissively licensed material, but each rests on a rekordbox behaviour that no source confirms and only the student's own Mac can settle. For the effects engine, the RMX-1000's four sections (Scene FX, Isolate FX, X-Pad FX, Release FX) reduce to a handful of textbook blocks: a filtered feedback delay, a state-variable filter, a Linkwitz-Riley band splitter, a Schroeder or plate reverb, a delay-line pitch shifter and a variable-rate ring buffer, all small enough to write from scratch inside one AudioWorklet. The audio path is rekordbox to BlackHole 2ch to Chrome to a sound card, with an untested planning estimate of **10 to 25 ms of added master latency**, and the cleanest route (processed audio leaving by the FLX2's own MASTER jack) depends on rekordbox accepting a macOS Aggregate Device while Hardware Unlock stays active. For the screen add-on, **rekordbox exposes no live deck data in Performance mode**, so the realistic choices are Mixxx (every field, open source, FLX4 supported, FLX2 through an unmerged mapping), a fragile memory-reading hybrid that keeps rekordbox, or a cropped screen capture that yields pixels only. Hardware is the easy part: a laptop-driven HDMI touch panel plus a $5 Pico 2 runs from roughly $60 to $265 across three tiers, and build123d let the research agent build, measure, interference-check, slice and unit-test an enclosure headlessly in seconds. Evidence quality is uneven and is labelled throughout: only GitHub, PyPI and npm pages and the container CAD tests are primary; all RMX-1000 manual wording, all rekordbox and Chrome documentation, and every price are search-snippet grade; and nothing was tested on a controller, a Mac or a printer.

## The RMX-1000 is four macro sections built from textbook blocks

Everything in this section is snippet grade unless marked. The unit samples at **48 kHz with 24-bit converters** ([Pioneer DJ archived specifications](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-gb/product/effector/archive/rmx-1000/black/specifications/)), routes its input through the Scene FX, Isolate FX and Release FX sections, and feeds the X-Pad's own output into the same three, so sampler and roll audio is itself processed ([manual extract hosted by Leisuretec](https://www.leisuretec.co.uk/product/rmx1000-black-professional-dj-effects-remix-workstation-webpio00074/NTgzYy9STVgtMTAwMCBVc2VyIE1hbnVhbC5wZGY=)). The order of Isolate and Scene in the chain was not recovered and is a design choice for an original engine. Each Scene FX is a macro: one large knob drives several parameters at once, with two sub-parameter knobs (SP1, SP2) trimming the result. The manual's rule is that Build Up effects add sound and Break Down effects cut it ([manualsdir p.15](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=15)).

One correction matters for scoping. The list "Roll, Trans, Add, Rev Delay, Offset" that appeared in this project's working brief as the X-Pad effects is the **RMX-500's Rhythm FX list**, not the RMX-1000's; on the RMX-1000, Trans, Roll and Add live inside the Isolate FX modes ([DJ TechTools RMX-500 review](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2014/03/09/review-pioneer-rmx-500-effects-unit/); [manualsdir p.17](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=17)). An original engine can sensibly take the union of both. Three things stay unknown after the search: the sub-parameters of BPF, HPF and LPF Echo and both reverbs; the X-Pad's beat divisions; and the isolator's crossover frequencies and slopes, which Pioneer does not appear to publish. The product itself is now archived, and AlphaTheta's successor, the RMX-IGNITE, drops Scene FX for lever and per-band effects ([AlphaTheta announcement](https://alphatheta.com/en/information/meet-the-next-gen-rmx-ignite-effector/)), which is a reminder that the layout is not sacred. Effect types such as echo, roll, gate and brake are generic; names such as "Scene FX", "X-Pad" and "remixbox" are AlphaTheta product terms, so the engine should carry its own names and parameter mappings.

### Port reverb, pitch and filters; write the rest

A browser constraint shapes the architecture: a native `DelayNode` inside a feedback cycle is clamped to at least one 128-frame render quantum, and native nodes cannot cheaply host a per-sample nonlinearity in the loop, so the whole echo belongs inside one `AudioWorkletProcessor` (recalled from the [W3C Web Audio specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#DelayNode)). Within that worklet, the licence picture sorts into three bands, all at snippet grade because no LICENSE file was opened.

### Tempo needs phase, and rekordbox will not supply it

The hardware measures BPM automatically over **70 to 180 BPM**, falls back to tap tempo, trims with Nudge, and uses Quantize to lock X-Pad sounds and Trans/Roll to the beat ([manualsdir p.11](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=11)). A reviewer found the auto BPM good but slow to follow large tempo changes and noted the unit cannot slave to incoming MIDI clock ([DJWorx review](https://djworx.com/review-pioneer-rmx-1000-remix-station/)). The canonical open algorithms are Scheirer's comb-filter bank, which finds tempo and beat phase causally ([Scheirer 1998](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/Schei98-beats.pdf)), Ellis's dynamic-programming tracker, which is offline by design ([Ellis 2007](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/zduan/teaching/ece472/reading/Ellis_2007.pdf)), and Percival and Tzanetakis's autocorrelation plus pulse-train scoring ([DBLP record](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/taslp/PercivalT14.html)). In JavaScript, realtime-bpm-analyzer is AudioWorklet-based and web-audio-beat-detector is MIT ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/web-audio-beat-detector)); the research judges that peak-interval detectors of this lineage give BPM but not reliable beat phase, which quantised effects need, so a pulse-train or comb stage is worth writing in-house.

Getting tempo from rekordbox instead is harder than it looks. rekordbox does not send MIDI clock; Pioneer staff said engineers were considering it with no timeline ([Pioneer community thread](https://community.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/22977420687001-Virtual-Midi-Clock-Send-in-Rekordbox)). It does support Ableton Link ([rekordbox Link FAQ](https://rekordbox.com/en/support/faq/ableton-link)), but a web page cannot join a Link session because discovery uses UDP multicast, so a local companion process must relay timing ([ricochet pull request](https://github.com/MohamedFekryyy/ricochet/pull/3)). Worse, in rekordbox Link is the tempo authority that decks follow, not a broadcast of the master deck, so a DJ who beatmatches by hand on the FLX2's pitch faders publishes nothing useful; that reading is an inference from user reports. The only tool that publishes true beat-grid phase, rkbx_link, reads rekordbox's process memory, is "somewhat experimental" on macOS, Apple Silicon only, and outputs Link, OSC and sACN rather than anything a browser reads directly (primary: [rkbx_link README](https://github.com/grufkork/rkbx_link)). The practical priority order is therefore audio beat tracking on the master signal the page already receives, tap tempo with a 0.1 BPM trim as the manual fallback, and Link through a companion only if the student is willing to play with decks slaved to Link.

## BlackHole routing works two ways, and 10 to 25 ms is an estimate

This section mixes grades: the BlackHole facts and one latency measurement are primary, the rekordbox and Chrome facts are snippet grade, and both step-by-step routes are untested syntheses.

### Chrome must be told to leave the audio alone

Capture needs `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` all false with `channelCount: 2`, followed by a read-back of `track.getSettings()`, because Chrome treats constraints as requests and forces mono when echo cancellation is on ([Addpipe blog](https://blog.addpipe.com/recording-true-stereo-audio-using-getusermedia/); [discuss-webrtc thread](https://groups.google.com/g/discuss-webrtc/c/ZoqwOw6ZjPg)). With processing off, Chrome reportedly passes through the device's native rate and ignores the sample-rate constraint ([public-webrtc-logs](https://lists.w3.org/Archives/Public/public-webrtc-logs/2023Apr/0082.html)), so the reliable way to avoid resampling is to set BlackHole, the controller and the `AudioContext` to one rate in Audio MIDI Setup. Output uses `AudioContext.setSinkId()` or the `sinkId` constructor option, available since **Chrome 110**, to send the page's audio to a specific device while capturing from another ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/audiocontext-setsinkid)). `latencyHint: 'interactive'` is already the default and the lowest setting. `baseLatency` and `outputLatency` are buffer-size estimates, not measurements of the real path (primary: [Web Audio issue 2467](https://github.com/WebAudio/web-audio-api/issues/2467)); no JavaScript API reports true end-to-end latency. A plain AudioWorklet in JavaScript or single-threaded WASM needs no cross-origin isolation; only `SharedArrayBuffer` does, and GitHub Pages cannot send the required headers without a service-worker shim ([GitHub community discussion](https://github.com/orgs/community/discussions/13309)), so parameters should travel by `AudioParam` and `postMessage` if the gesture app is hosted there.

### Two routes: one documented, one cleaner

BlackHole 2ch is the virtual cable: GPL-3.0, installable with `brew install blackhole-2ch`, 44.1 and 48 kHz among its rates, and "zero additional driver latency" by its own claim (primary: [BlackHole README](https://github.com/ExistentialAudio/BlackHole)). Loopback is the paid alternative at about $99 ([Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/opinion/streaming-on-my-mac-shouldnt-require-this-amazing-dollar99-app-but-it-does)).

**Route A uses rekordbox's PC MASTER OUT** and is the better documented. A Pioneer forum guide shows a controller plus BlackHole working this way ([Pioneer forum streaming guide](https://forums.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/articles/115005326586-Streaming-with-OBS-DDJ-RB-Mac)), and with an FLX4 the Master Output reads "DDJ-FLX4 : MASTER + computer audio device" ([We Are Crossfader FLX4 guide](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/getting-started-with-the-pioneer-dj-ddj-flx4/)). The catch is that every source shows the master duplicated to the controller, so the dry signal still plays from the controller's MASTER jack. The processed signal must therefore leave by a different output, such as the Mac's headphone jack or a second DAC, and must never be sent back to the controller's channels 1 and 2, where it would sum with the dry master. The controller's jack becomes a dry, zero-latency booth feed.

**Route B uses an Aggregate Device** containing the controller as clock source and BlackHole with drift correction on, following Apple's guidance for unsynchronised devices ([Apple Support](https://support.apple.com/en-mo/102171)). rekordbox selects the aggregate, sends Master to the BlackHole pair and Headphones to the controller's channels 3 and 4, and the page outputs to the raw controller device so its stereo output lands on the MASTER jack. Pioneer staff describe exactly this aggregate pattern, but for a Focusrite interface, not a controller ([Pioneer community thread](https://community.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/22977946513561-Rekordbox-Cue-Sound-Output)). The FLX2 does enumerate four output channels, since a Mixxx user assigned main to 1 and 2 and headphones to 3 and 4 ([Mixxx forum](https://mixxx.discourse.group/t/mixxx-ddj-flx2-headphone-output-issue/32696)). Route B gives one cable from controller to speakers but hangs on two unverified behaviours: that rekordbox lists the aggregate with a controller attached, and that Hardware Unlock still fires when the controller is not the selected audio device. One user report adds that the aggregate must be named exactly "Aggregate Device" ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/cant-record-sound-rekordbox-blackhole.163101/)).

### Expect a delay like standing seven metres from the speakers

The render quantum is 128 frames, 2.67 ms at 48 kHz ([W3C Web Audio API 1.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio-1.1/)). A configurable `renderSizeHint` had an Intent to Ship for Chrome 153, and one article reports a 64-frame quantum shortening a physical loopback by about 2.9 ms ([Classmethod](https://dev.classmethod.jp/en/articles/chrome-153-webaudio-render-size-hint-latency/)); whether it is in stable Chrome and honoured on macOS is unconfirmed. The only hardware-measured figure found is **19 to 23 ms round trip** on macOS through a Focusrite interface with a 128-sample buffer, in both Chrome and Firefox, and that figure includes an analogue input stage a virtual cable does not have (primary: [WAlatencyCompensation](https://github.com/micbuffa/WAlatencyCompensation)). From that, the research estimates the browser insert at roughly 10 to 25 ms. This is an estimate, not a measurement; no published figure exists for Chrome through a virtual device to a USB DJ controller.

That delay applies only to the room. The headphone cue path bypasses the browser, so beatmatching in headphones is unaffected, but the DJ also never hears the effects in headphones and must audition them from the speakers. Mixxx's guidance treats 36 to 64 ms as acceptable for controller use and recommends under 16 ms for vinyl control ([Mixxx wiki manual](https://github.com/mixxxdj/mixxx/wiki/manual)), and by simple arithmetic 20 ms equals about 6.9 m of speaker distance, a situation club DJs already handle with headphones. Blend mixing survives this; scratching or fast cuts judged from the main speakers do not. The alternative of a native AU or VST host reading BlackHole would plausibly cut the insert to single-digit milliseconds, but that figure is an unsourced estimate and abandons the zero-install web app. Windows is less favourable: VB-Cable alone adds up to about 21 ms with a 2048-sample internal buffer ([VB-Audio forum](https://forum.vb-audio.com/viewtopic.php?p=2392)).

### What the Free plan allows

The FLX2 is USB class-compliant ([VirtualDJ FLX2 manual](https://virtualdj.com/manuals/hardware/alphatheta/ddjflx2/setup.html)) and is listed as a Hardware Unlock device, which gives Performance mode without a paid plan ([rekordbox Hardware Unlock](https://rekordbox.com/en/support/hardware-unlock)). The unlock list in the rekordbox 7 FAQ includes Performance mode control, Recording and **MIDI Learn** ([rekordbox 7 FAQ](https://rekordbox.com/en/support/faq/v7/)), which matters because a second MIDI device could then be mapped alongside the controller. The account still shows "Free", which is normal ([Pioneer community answer](https://community.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/45283204112281/comments/45324662933145)). Audio preferences expose a device selector, Mixer Mode and separate Master and Headphones output channels ([rekordbox troubleshooting FAQ](https://rekordbox.com/en/support/faq/trouble-shooting-5/)), and no source shows a rule that fixes the audio device when a controller is attached, though that is absence of evidence. Unconfirmed for the Free plan on version 7: Ableton Link, stems, and whether the FLX2's sample rate and bit depth combinations match the service manual's "44.1 kHz / 48 kHz, 16 bit / 24 bit" line ([Pioneer service manual page](https://docs.pioneerdj.com/manuals/SM_DDJ_FLX2_SM_manual/?Page=2)). A reader comment claiming the FLX2 is no longer an unlock device contradicts rekordbox's own list and is unverified.

## Rekordbox exposes no live deck data, so Mixxx wins on data

The GitHub-hosted sources in this section are primary; Mixxx manual pages, DJ TechTools articles and the EULA are snippet grade; no path was bench-tested. rekordbox has no official live-data output in Performance mode: a feature request for master-deck track info is still open on Pioneer's forum ([Pioneer community](https://community.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/37818300129689-In-Rekordbox-output-the-track-info-of-the-current-master-deck)), and Pro DJ Link libraries cannot help because "controllers which don't even have Link (Ethernet) jacks can't work" ([beat-link README](https://github.com/Deep-Symmetry/beat-link)). Every working rekordbox project either reads process memory or reads files rekordbox writes. No project was found that shows live rekordbox Performance-mode waveforms on an external device; every DIY screen build found runs Mixxx or listens to real CDJs.

The rekordbox hybrid deserves a clear-eyed look because it is the only route to true data without leaving rekordbox. rkbx_link's recent commits are almost all per-version offset updates, several inside ten weeks in mid-2026, so a display built on it breaks at each rekordbox auto-update until new offsets ship ([rkbx_link commits](https://github.com/grufkork/rkbx_link/commits/master)). Current offsets require a paid licence, listed at EUR 25 ([Gumroad](https://griffindigicoustics.gumroad.com/l/rkbx_link)). On macOS the setup strips Apple notarization from rekordbox.app and adds the `get-task-allow` entitlement, which its own documentation notes "allows library injection if needed" ([MACOS_SETUP.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/grufkork/rkbx_link/master/MACOS_SETUP.md)). The rekordbox licence agreement bars reverse engineering ([rekordbox EULA](https://rekordbox.com/en/license-agreement/), snippet grade), so memory reading, database decryption and re-signing sit against it, while Mixxx, MIDI listening, screen capture and parsing unencrypted analysis files do not. For a personal build that is the same position as the public projects; a distributed product would need legal advice. The waveform itself is the well-understood part: rekordbox's ANLZ files hold beat grids, cue lists and several waveform encodings, and pyrekordbox parses them under an MIT licence, though it lists the CDJ-3000-era three-band tags PWV6 and PWV7 as unsupported ([pyrekordbox ANLZ documentation](https://pyrekordbox.readthedocs.io/en/stable/formats/anlz.html)).

The research's ranking follows directly. If the host can be Mixxx, build on the scripted control path now and compute the waveform separately, with the QML renderer or a fork as the later upgrade; TriMixxx, a DIY CDJ on a Mixxx 2.5.6 fork with a single-deck skin, shows the approach works ([DJ TechTools](https://djtechtools.com/2026/10/16/trimixxx-is-a-diy-cdj-built-on-a-raspberry-pi/)). If rekordbox must stay, prototype the screen capture first because it works today and never breaks on an update, and treat the memory-reading hybrid as an optional data mode. Designing the screen to accept either video frames or a compact deck-state protocol lets both share one renderer.

## A laptop-driven HDMI panel beats smarter hardware at every tier

Everything here is snippet grade, and prices are volatile 2026 snapshots that exclude most tariffs and shipping. The professional reference is modest: the CDJ-3000 has a 9-inch "HD" touch screen, reported as 720p and not multi-touch ([DJ Mag](https://djmag.com/news/pioneer-dj-introduce-their-next-gen-player-cdj-3000)), and the CDJ-3000X a 10.1-inch 1280x800 panel ([AlphaTheta CDJ-3000X](https://alphatheta.com/en/product/player/cdj-3000x/)). A commodity HDMI touch panel is already in that class, and single-point touch is no downgrade.

| Tier | Display | Compute and controls | Estimated total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Prototype | A phone or tablet the student already owns, as a browser client of a laptop-hosted page | Raspberry Pi Pico 2 at $5 and an EC11 push encoder at $1.60 to $4.25 ([Pico 2](https://raspberrypi.com/products/raspberry-pi-pico-2/); [RobotShop EC11](https://robotshop.com/en/ec11-rotary-encoder-module.html)) | $10 to $15 |

The totals include unsourced estimates for cables, buttons, a hub and a Pi power supply. The default recommendation is the "dumb display" at every tier because the laptop already holds the track data and has the stronger GPU: an HDMI panel refreshes at its native rate with no boot time, no sync protocol and no firmware. Single-board computers roughly doubled in price during 2026, so a smart display adds $60 to $130 and about 20 seconds of boot ([Core Electronics](https://core-electronics.com.au/guides/raspberry-pi-5-review-performance-and-benchmarks/)) for no benefit unless laptop-free playback becomes a goal. Microcontroller boards are tempting on price but an ESP32-S3 benchmarks at about 25 fps at 800x480 where an ESP32-P4 reaches about 60 fps ([Espressif LCD application note](https://docs.espressif.com/projects/esp-techpedia/en/latest/esp-friends/advanced-development/lcd-application-note/overview.html)), and either forces the whole interface to be written as firmware. On fit, the 11.26-inch bar's active width of about 279 mm spans roughly 73% of an FLX2 and 58% of an FLX4 by the researcher's arithmetic, leaving room for an encoder beside it; its 64 mm height suits two stacked waveforms but not a browse list, which is the 12.3-inch bar's advantage.

For controls, a Pico 2 enumerating as class-compliant USB MIDI is the standard answer, with the Control Surface library handling relative-CC encoders ([Control Surface](https://github.com/tttapa/Control-Surface)); MIDI beats a HID keyboard because DJ software can MIDI-learn it and no stray keystrokes reach the focused window. Power is the trap. Both controllers are rated at 5 V, 500 mA on bus power ([FLX2 spec sheet](https://cdn.teufelaudio.com/products/AlphaTheta/DDJ-FLX2/DDJ-FLX2-spec-sheet-HR); [Pioneer DJ FLX4 specifications](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-us/product/controller/ddj-flx4/black/specifications/)), the one bar display with a published figure draws about another 500 mA at full brightness ([The Pi Hut](https://thepihut.com/products/8-8-ips-hdmi-side-monitor-480x1920)), and a USB 3 port defaults to 900 mA ([TI TUSB320 datasheet](https://ti.com/document-viewer/TUSB320/datasheet/GUID-3BF156CA-3E11-436F-8D41-07D491DBA492)). The safe default is the controller on its own laptop port, since it carries the audio interface, and the screen unit on a second port or a hub with its own power input. Most HDMI bars also need two cables, and Waveshare documents touch for Windows and Linux only, so macOS touch behaviour is unknown until tried.

## build123d passes headless checks; calipers and coupons stay human

### The agent's toolchain was run, not just read about

This subsection is primary: the researcher executed it in an Ubuntu 24.04 container with Python 3.13 and no GPU. **build123d 0.13.0** (Apache-2.0, released 2026-09-21) installed with one `pip` command in under a minute ([PyPI build123d](https://pypi.org/project/build123d/)). A filleted, shelled 120x80x30 mm box with a port cut-out and lipped lid built in 0.17 s and exported STEP, STL and a two-part 3MF. The same Python process returned bounding box, volume, centre of mass, validity and manifold status, and a boolean intersection between lid and body found a real 0.48 mm³ clash where the lid's square lip met the body's rounded corners. trimesh confirmed the STL watertight ([PyPI trimesh](https://pypi.org/project/trimesh/)), admesh agreed, PrusaSlicer's command line sliced it without a display and reported 3 h 49 min and 40.6 cm³ of filament ([PrusaSlicer](https://github.com/prusa3d/PrusaSlicer)), and a five-assertion pytest file covering bed fit, outer dimensions, validity, watertightness and mass passed in 1.5 s. Only PNG rendering needed a virtual display: PyVista failed bare and succeeded under `xvfb-run -a` ([PyPI pyvista](https://pypi.org/project/pyvista/)).

One trap surfaced in testing: installing CadQuery 2.8.0 into the same virtualenv overwrote the shared OCP kernel module and broke build123d, so the two must live in separate environments ([PyPI cadquery](https://pypi.org/project/cadquery/)). OpenSCAD is the weaker choice for this project: Ubuntu's package is still the 2021.01 stable release ([Ubuntu package](https://packages.ubuntu.com/noble/openscad)), with no STEP export and no native fillets per the researcher's background knowledge. build123d is also the default backend of CADGenBench, the one neutral benchmark that tests iterative agent loops ([CADGenBench](https://github.com/huggingface/cadgenbench)). Ready-made agent tooling exists but was not installed: build123d-mcp offers execute, render, measure, validate and export tools plus Claude skills ([build123d-mcp](https://github.com/pzfreo/build123d-mcp)), earthtojake/text-to-cad is a large MIT skill pack on build123d ([text-to-cad](https://github.com/earthtojake/text-to-cad)), and flowful-ai/cad-skill is useful but carries a non-commercial licence ([cad-skill](https://github.com/flowful-ai/cad-skill)). The research's design rule is to build the workflow around a verify loop rather than one-shot generation, to pin `build123d==0.13.0` because its API moves between versions, and to model the controller as a keep-out solid so that "does it fit the controller" becomes an automated assertion.

### Controller dimensions are published; mounting geometry is not

The controller facts are snippet grade. The **DDJ-FLX4 measures 482 x 272.8 x 59.2 mm and weighs 2.1 kg** ([Pioneer DJ FLX4 specifications](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-us/product/controller/ddj-flx4/black/specifications/)); its rear carries two USB-C ports, RCA master outputs, a quarter-inch mic input and a Kensington slot ([DDJ-FLX4 manual](https://assets.ctfassets.net/q602vtcuu3w3/2vz7GMLHawLZBK6gWpCP9V/26bd62eef3eca3714ddb1d3396d30f7b/DDJ-FLX4_manual_EN.pdf)). The **DDJ-FLX2 measures 383.2 x 208 x 48.2 mm and weighs 1.2 kg** ([FLX2 spec sheet](https://cdn.teufelaudio.com/products/AlphaTheta/DDJ-FLX2/DDJ-FLX2-spec-sheet-HR)); its rear has only the USB-C port and Kensington slot, and its two 3.5 mm jacks sit on the left side ([DJ Life Mag review](https://djlifemag.com/2025/07/ddj-flx2-doing-a-lot-with-a-little-review/)). Neither has documented mounting holes, and no dimensioned STEP model of either was found; the models on sale are render assets. The published heights most likely include the tallest control, so chassis-top height, port positions, foot positions and under-chassis clearance are all unmeasured. Sources also conflict on where the FLX4's headphone jack sits.

Those gaps drive the mounting choice. The research recommends a free-standing rear bridge whose feet carry thin tongues that slide under the controller's rear corners, so the controller's own weight resists tipping and nothing clamps the plastic chassis; the fallback is printing only a display tray for a bought laptop stand. This is engineering judgement, supported by community precedents such as a slide-under laptop stand and a phone-mount remix for the FLX4 ([MakerWorld 1398110](https://makerworld.com/models/1398110); [MakerWorld 2930023](https://makerworld.com/models/2930023)), which are also the best available fit references. Constraints to encode: leave the FLX4's whole rear face open with room for long RCA plugs, keep the FLX2's left side clear for its audio cables, treat the Kensington slot as a locating feature only, and check that a Decksaver cover, at 486 x 270 x 36 mm on the FLX4, still fits ([Thomann](https://www.thomann.co.uk/decksaver_pioneer_dj_ddj_flx4.htm)). Both controllers are wider than a 256 mm print bed, so any full-width beam splits into two or three pieces or becomes an aluminium tube.

### Beginner skills, GUI CAD and getting parts made

For the human side, Onshape is the fastest start on macOS: it runs in a browser, its free tier allows unlimited public documents for non-commercial use, and an education programme exists for qualifying students ([Onshape help](https://cad.onshape.com/help/Content/Plans/managing_your_onshape_account_and_subscription.htm)). Fusion for personal use is the close second, free for non-commercial projects with ten active editable documents ([Autodesk](https://www.autodesk.com/products/fusion-360/blog/the-10-document-limit-for-personal-use-explained-in-7-steps/)), and FreeCAD 1.1.2 is the fully free offline fallback ([FreeCAD blog](https://blog.freecad.org/2026/07/23/freecad-1-1-2-released/)). Tinkercad lacks a general fillet tool ([Instructables](https://www.instructables.com/id/Filleting-Taking-the-Edge-Off/)) and Shapr3D's free tier exports only low-resolution meshes ([Shapr3D subscription plans](https://support.shapr3d.com/hc/en-us/articles/7871961422364)), so neither suits a fitted enclosure. The practical workflow is hybrid: the agent emits STEP from build123d and the student opens it in Onshape or Fusion to inspect and measure. The skills that cannot be delegated are caliper measurement and tolerance thinking, slicer literacy, and printing test coupons; basic parametric modelling, soldering, heat-set insert installation and connector strain relief round out the list.

Design-for-printing defaults from the sources: 2.0 to 2.4 mm walls and 0.5 mm clearance around internal components ([Hubs enclosure guide](https://www.hubs.com/knowledge-base/enclosure-design-3d-printing-step-step-guide/)); 0.15 to 0.3 mm per-side clearance for sliding fits depending on the printer ([UAL fab-lab guide](https://wiki.cci.arts.ac.uk/books/digital-fabrication-lab/page/design-the-clearance-and-tolerance)); a 4.0 mm pilot hole for most M3 heat-set inserts ([Accu](https://accu-components.com/p/488-threaded-insert-hole-size-charts-for-3d-printing-pla-petg-resin)), with charts disagreeing for M2.5; and PETG over PLA because PLA softens near 55 to 60 °C while PETG holds to roughly 75 to 85 °C ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/pla-petg-glass-transition-temperature-3d-printing/)). Every one of these should be a named parameter confirmed by a single printed coupon. For fabrication, a university makerspace is almost certainly cheapest, since PETG filament averages about EUR 19.38 per kilogram ([printer-hub price index](https://printer-hub.ru/en/posts/3d-printing-service-prices)). Online, JLC3DP published MJF nylon at $0.275 per gram from August 2025 ([JLC3DP](https://jlc3dp.com/blog/drastic-material-price-cuts-and-adjusted-post-processing-service-fees)), which the research converts to an estimated $52 to $72 for a 300 x 100 x 60 mm shell before shipping; no real quote was obtained, and Craftcloud compares vendors in real time ([All3DP](https://all3dp.com/2/best-cheap-3d-printing-services/)). Services that accept STEP are another reason to prefer build123d over a mesh-only tool.

## Conclusion

The two projects share one lesson: the published, verifiable parts are the DSP, the CAD toolchain and the hardware, while the uncertain parts all sit at the boundary with rekordbox. That inverts the usual order of work. The effects engine can be developed and auditioned against any audio source today, but whether it becomes a master insert on the FLX2's own output depends on a preference panel nobody has documented with a controller attached. The screen is cheap to build and easy to verify in code, but what it can show depends on choosing between rekordbox loyalty and data access, and Mixxx is the only host where the answer is not a workaround. A single web renderer served from the laptop links the two projects more tightly than the hardware does: it prototypes on a phone, runs on an HDMI bar, and shares the gesture app's deployment.

Before any hands-on work, three desk checks cost nothing: read the RMX-1000 manual's effect pages directly, open the LICENSE file of every project intended for porting, and confirm on rekordbox.com which features the version 7 Free plan includes. Everything else below needs the real equipment.
