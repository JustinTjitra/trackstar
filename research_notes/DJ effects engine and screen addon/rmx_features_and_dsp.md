## 1. What are the RMX-1000's four sections and what does each control do?

### Takeaway
The four sections and the Scene FX (5 Build Up + 5 Break Down), Isolate FX (4 modes) and Release FX (3 types) lists in the brief are confirmed by manual extracts. **The X-Pad FX list in the brief is wrong for the RMX-1000**: "Roll, Trans, Add, Rev Delay, Offset" is the *RMX-500's* Rhythm FX list; the RMX-1000's X-Pad FX section is ROLL plus four sampler slots (KICK, SNARE, CLAP, HI HAT) with OVERDUB, HOLD/MUTE, BANK and PITCH.

### Cited Findings

#### Overall architecture and signal flow
- The manual has separate operating sections for SCENE FX, ISOLATE FX, X-PAD FX and RELEASE FX, each followed by a "Types of effects" list and (in the remixbox manual) a parameter adjustment range list. — [Pioneer remixbox software manual TOC](https://docs.pioneerdj.com/Manuals/RMX_1000_DRQ1001A_EN_software_manual/?Page=2); [manualsdir mirror p.4](https://manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=4)
- Routing: the sound at the INPUT terminals is routed to the SCENE FX, ISOLATE FX and RELEASE FX sections; the X-PAD output is routed into the same three sections (i.e. sampler/roll audio is itself processed by Scene/Isolate/Release). — [manual extract via leisuretec-hosted user manual](https://www.leisuretec.co.uk/product/rmx1000-black-professional-dj-effects-remix-workstation-webpio00074/NTgzYy9STVgtMTAwMCBVc2VyIE1hbnVhbC5wZGY=)
- Audio specs: 48 kHz sampling, 24-bit A/D and D/A, 20 Hz–20 kHz, THD ≤ 0.005 %. — [Pioneer DJ archived specifications page](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-gb/product/effector/archive/rmx-1000/black/specifications/). S/N 102 dB (SEND/RETURN, −10 dB mode) / 98 dB (MASTER, +4 dB mode), 20 dB headroom. — [ProSoundGear listing](https://prosoundgear.com/shop/dj-gear/effect-processors/pioneer-rmx-1000-remix-station/)
- I/O listed by retailers: 1/4-inch and RCA line in/out plus USB-B; fully MIDI-assignable over USB. — [Soundpro](https://www.soundpro.com/pioneer-dj-rmx-1000-remix-station/); [Digital DJ Tips review](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/pioneer-rmx-1000/)

#### ISOLATE FX (three knobs LOW / MID / HI; mode selector with 4 types)
- Confirmed modes: **ISOLATOR, CUT/ADD, TRANS/ROLL, GATE/DRIVE**; operation is "turn the [LOW], [MID] or [HI] control"; each knob acts on its own frequency band. — [manualsdir p.12](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=12); [manualsdir p.16 "Types of isolate fx effects"](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=16); [Pioneer product page](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en/product/effector/rmx-1000/white/overview/)
- ISOLATOR: per-band output level control for low, mid and high. — [Pioneer product page](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en/product/effector/rmx-1000/white/overview/)
- CUT/ADD: "CUT: when turned counterclockwise" – the manual says this adjusts the input's bass drum level and the manual filter's cutoff frequency; reviewers describe it as HPF/LPF-style cutting to the left "while still keeping the kick drum in the mix" and an echo/delay added to that band when turned right (ADD). — [official manual PDF](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/88481.pdf); reviews: [ask.audio](https://ask.audio/articles/review-pioneer-rmx-1000), [Juno Daily](https://www.juno.co.uk/junodaily/2012/07/03/pioneer-rmx1000-remix-station-review), [macProVideo](https://www.macprovideo.com/article/review-2/review-pioneer-rmx-1000/how-it-works) (exact per-review attribution not confirmed)
- TRANS/ROLL: counterclockwise = TRANS, "cuts the input sound according to the multiplying factor of the beat" set by knob position (beat-synced gate/transformer on that band); clockwise = ROLL, "records the input sound starting from the point when the [LOW], [MID] or [HI] control is turned" and repeats it at the beat multiple (per-band loop roll). — [manualslib p.18](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/681089/Pioneer-Remix-Station-Rmx-1000.html?page=18); [manualsdir p.17](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=17)
- GATE/DRIVE: "GATE: when turned counterclockwise"; reviews: gate applied to the band when turned left, band "driven" (overdrive/distortion) when turned right. — [official manual PDF](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/88481.pdf); [MusicRadar review](https://www.musicradar.com/reviews/tech/pioneer-rmx-1000-remix-station-561298)

#### X-PAD FX (touch strip + buttons)
- Controls confirmed from manual extracts: **[ROLL]**, four sampler buttons **[KICK] [SNARE] [CLAP] [HI HAT]**, **[OVERDUB (DELETE)]**, **[HOLD (MUTE)]**, **[BANK]**, **PITCH** knob (sends MIDI CC 5, 0–127). — [manualsdir p.13](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=13); [manualslib MIDI assignment map p.22](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1057095/Pioneer-Rmx-1000.html?page=22)
- ROLL: press [ROLL] so it lights, touch the X-PAD; "the sound at the time the X-PAD is touched is sampled and loop playback starts"; the number of beats sampled depends on the touch position. — [manualslib.es p.13](https://manualslib.es/manual/7919/Pioneer-Rmx-1000.html?page=13); corroborated ("rolls audio at different note values depending on finger position") by [Digital DJ Tips](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/reviews/pioneer-rmx-1000/)
- Sampler: select a sound button, touch the pad; the sound is added in beat with the track's rhythm, changes with finger position and stops when the finger is released; "the X-PAD FX output is synchronized with the tempo of the currently playing track". — [manualsdir p.13](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=13)
- OVERDUB: records four beats of the sounds assigned to the X-PAD FX buttons and loops them; pressing further buttons layers sounds; pressing OVERDUB again stops and deletes. OVERDUB(DELETE)+button removes that layer (not recoverable); HOLD(MUTE)+button mutes/unmutes a layer. — [manualsdir p.13](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=13)
- HOLD: keeps the effect that was active when the finger left the pad; press again to cancel. — [manualsdir p.13](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=13)
- BANK: BANK + sampler button selects/loads a sampled sound (from SD card); sampler bank is described as 4×4; user samples are 48 kHz 24-bit or 16-bit WAV, prepared with remixbox. — [manualsdir p.13](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=13); [jamhire listing](https://jamhire.co.uk/pioneer-rmx-1000%EF%BF%BC)
- **Correction to the brief**: no search of the RMX-1000 manual returned "REV DELAY" or "OFFSET" under X-PAD FX. Those names belong to the **RMX-500** "Rhythm FX" section: audio effects Roll, Trans, Add (multi-tap delay), Rev Delay, Offset ("a kind of beatmasher") plus drum buttons Kick, Snare, Clap, Hi Hat, Cymbal, with a 4-beat sequencer/overdub. — [DJ TechTools RMX-500 review](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2014/03/09/review-pioneer-rmx-500-effects-unit/); [RMX-500 manual p.11 mirror](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486605/pioneer-rmx-500.html?page=11); [Adorama RMX-500](https://www.adorama.com/pirmx500.html)
- On the RMX-1000, TRANS and ROLL exist as per-band Isolate FX (TRANS/ROLL mode) and ADD as the clockwise half of CUT/ADD — not as X-Pad effects. — [manualsdir p.17](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=17)

#### RELEASE FX (spring lever + 3-way selector)
- Types confirmed: **VINYL BRAKE, ECHO, BACK SPIN**, chosen with a selector switch. — [manualslib p.18 "Types of release fx effects"](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/681089/Pioneer-Remix-Station-Rmx-1000.html?page=18)
- Lever: pulling the lever toward you turns the effect on, and the effect changes with lever position; releasing it stops the effect and also cuts off any active SCENE FX, ISOLATE FX and X-PAD FX. — [manualslib.mx p.14 "Release FX section"](https://manualslib.mx/manual/7919/Pioneer-Rmx-1000.html?page=14); [official manual PDF](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/88481.pdf)
- BACK SPIN is described as the input sound "played in reverse at high speed". — [manualslib p.18](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/681089/Pioneer-Remix-Station-Rmx-1000.html?page=18)
- Reviewer framing: Release FX is used either to remove the music and leave only the effect tail, or to remove all effects so the clean track continues. — [DJ TechTools RMX-1000 announcement](https://djtechtools.com/amp/2012/03/16/pioneer-rmx-1000-announced-new-remixsampling-device); [Gear4music listing](https://www.gear4music.com/us/en/PA-DJ-and-Lighting/Pioneer-RMX-1000-Effector-Black/JAD)

#### remixbox / user settings
- remixbox (bundled editor) edits the SCENE FX, ISOLATE FX, X-PAD FX and RELEASE FX sections and sampler content, with a parameter adjustment range list per section; data goes to an SD card and is used by setting the [SETTING] switch to [USER]. — [remixbox software manual](https://docs.pioneerdj.com/Manuals/RMX_1000_DRQ1001A_EN_software_manual/?Page=2); [Pioneer product page](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en/product/effector/rmx-1000/black/overview/)

### Inferences
- The brief's X-Pad list conflates two products. For an original engine the union is a reasonable generic target: {roll, trans, add (multi-tap delay), reverse delay, offset/beat-masher} as pad/rhythm effects (RMX-500 style) plus a 4-slot one-shot sampler with a 4-beat overdub loop (RMX-1000 style). None of these names are needed; all are generic effect types.
- Every Scene FX is a macro: one knob drives several parameters at once (filter cutoff + echo send/feedback + level), which is why the manual descriptions are terse. Break Down effects that "cut sound" most plausibly reduce the dry signal toward silence as the knob rises while the wet tail takes over; Build Up effects add wet/noise on top of dry. This is an interpretation of "Build Up adds / Break Down cuts", not a manual statement.
- "Sub Parameter 2 applies a modulation effect" on NOISE and CRUSH ECHO suggests an LFO on filter cutoff or amplitude (possibly beat-synced); the manual extract does not say which.
- SPIRAL = feedback delay whose repeats are progressively pitch-shifted (up or down) with the delay time itself tied to the knob, so moving the knob also produces tape-style pitch glide. This matches "when the delay time is changed, the pitch changes simultaneously" plus "amount of pitch shifting by which the pitch is raised/decreased".
- Since X-Pad audio feeds Scene/Isolate/Release, a faithful signal graph is: `input (+ X-Pad roll/sampler mix) -> Isolate FX (3-band) -> Scene FX -> Release FX -> output`. The order of Isolate vs Scene was not recovered; treat as a design choice.

### Gaps
- Full verbatim parameter tables (SP1/SP2 for BPF ECHO, ECHO, HPF ECHO, LPF ECHO, REVERB UP/DOWN) were not recoverable through search extracts. Needs a direct read of manual pp.15–17 (https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/88481.pdf or docs.pioneerdj.com).
- Exact ISOLATOR range (e.g. −∞ to +x dB), crossover frequencies and filter slopes: not found in any source. Pioneer does not appear to publish them.
- DRIVE (clockwise) wording in the manual, and what CUT does per band (the "bass drum level" sentence presumably applies to LOW only): not confirmed.
- X-PAD touch-strip beat divisions (e.g. 1/8 … 1/1), whether there is a separate X-PAD level control, and sampler pattern behaviour vs position: not confirmed.
- Release FX time/beat-length settings and exact dry/wet behaviour while the lever is held: sources are loosely worded and partly contradictory (one extract says the original sound drops out leaving only the effect; a retailer says releasing the lever removes effects while music continues). Both can be true (hold = effect, release = clean), but this is not verified verbatim.
- remixbox's exact customisable parameter list/ranges: the manual section exists but its contents were not read.
- RMX-IGNITE details come from launch coverage only; no manual was consulted.

## 2. How does the RMX-1000 get tempo and how are effects beat-synced?

### Takeaway
Tempo comes from automatic BPM measurement of the incoming audio (70–180 BPM) or manual TAP entry, trimmed with NUDGE; a QUANTIZE function locks X-Pad sounds and TRANS/ROLL timing to the detected beat. MIDI-wise the unit acts as a timing-clock source and USB controller (and syncs to a DAW host in plug-in mode); reviewers report it cannot slave to incoming MIDI clock.

### Cited Findings
- AUTO: BPM is measured automatically over a 70–180 BPM range; if auto measurement fails, press [AUTO] until [TAP] is displayed and tap the beat. — [manualsdir p.11 "Inputting the BPM manually"](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=11); [Pioneer DJ archived specs](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-gb/product/effector/archive/rmx-1000/black/specifications/)
- TAP: tapping two or more times sets the BPM to the average tap interval; holding [TAP] and pressing [AUTO] adjusts in 0.1 BPM steps. — [manualsdir p.11](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=11)
- QUANTIZE: "synchronizes the cycle and timing of X-PAD FX sounds and the TRANS/ROLL effect with the tempo"; NUDGE (+/−) shifts the timing forward/back for fine alignment (NUDGE and X-Pad position are sent as MIDI CCs). — [manualsdir p.11 "Using the nudge function"](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=11); [manualslib MIDI map p.22](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1057095/Pioneer-Rmx-1000.html?page=22)
- Echo-type Scene FX are output "several times according to the multiplying factor of the input sound's beat", i.e. delay times are beat fractions/multiples of the current BPM. — [manualsdir p.16 (Crush Echo)](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=16)
- MIDI: the manual instructs "set the MIDI sequencer's sync mode to Slave" and notes that devices not supporting MIDI timing clock cannot be synchronized — i.e. the RMX-1000 *sends* timing clock. — [official manual PDF](https://www.bhphotovideo.com/lit_files/88481.pdf); user confirmation of use as clock master: [Pioneer DJ forum "Using RMX1000 as midi clock master"](https://forums.pioneerdj.com/hc/en-us/community/posts/203046529--SOLVED-Using-RMX1000-as-midi-clock-master)
- Reviewer: "the lack of MIDI sync" (incoming clock from a DVS does nothing); auto BPM is good but slow to follow large tempo changes. In plug-in (VST/AU) mode the unit can follow the DAW host tempo while linked to a plug-in instance. — [DJWorx review](https://djworx.com/review-pioneer-rmx-1000-remix-station/)
- Community workaround for Serato sync routes through Ableton + Ableton Link + the RMX plug-in, and needs re-syncing after big tempo changes. — [Serato forum](https://serato.com/forum/discussion/1586645)
- MIDI beat clock runs at 24 pulses per quarter note. — [Wikipedia: MIDI beat clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIDI_beat_clock)

### Inferences
- A software equivalent needs three tempo sources with a priority order: (1) host/deck tempo and beat phase when the app already knows them (far better than the hardware's situation), (2) live auto-detection from audio constrained to roughly 70–180 BPM with octave folding (halve/double into range), (3) tap tempo = mean of the last N inter-tap intervals with outlier reset, plus ±0.1 BPM trim and a phase "nudge".
- Beat sync reduces to two quantities: beat period `T = 60 / BPM` seconds and beat phase `phi in [0,1)`. Delay times are `T * ratio` (1/8, 1/4, 1/2, 3/4, 1, 2…); gates/rolls/sampler triggers are scheduled on phase boundaries; "quantize" means deferring a pad action to the next subdivision boundary (or snapping the roll capture start to the most recent one).
- For external sync in a browser, MIDI clock in/out is possible via the Web MIDI API (24 PPQN; average several ticks to de-jitter). Ableton Link has no browser implementation, so it would need a native helper.

### Gaps
- Pioneer does not document its beat-detection algorithm; nothing public was found on how the RMX-1000 estimates beat *phase* (as opposed to BPM).
- Whether firmware updates ever added MIDI-clock slave mode: not found.
- Exact list of beat ratios available per effect: not recovered from the manual.

## 3. Standard DSP approach for each effect category

### Takeaway
Every RMX-style effect maps onto a small set of public, textbook building blocks: an interpolated circular delay line with a filter and saturator in the feedback path, a TPT/trapezoidal state-variable filter, a Linkwitz-Riley band splitter, a Freeverb/Dattorro/FDN reverb, a delay-line pitch shifter, and a recorded ring buffer with a variable-rate read head. An AudioWorklet implementation in TypeScript can build all of them from first principles without copying any GPL code.

### Cited Findings

#### Tempo-synced echo with a filter in the loop (BPF/HPF/LPF/Crush Echo, Release Echo, "Add")
- Tone.js `FeedbackDelay` is "a DelayNode in which part of the output signal is fed back into the delay"; `PingPongDelay` alternates the echo between channels — the minimal native-node version of the structure. — [Tone.js FeedbackDelay docs](https://tonejs.github.io/docs/15.0.4/classes/FeedbackDelay.html); [Tone.js PingPongDelay docs](https://tonejs.github.io/docs/r13/PingPongDelay)
- Julius O. Smith's *Physical Audio Signal Processing* covers delay lines, fractional-delay interpolation (linear, allpass, Lagrange) and comb filters; the lowpass-feedback comb filter (a delay with a one-pole lowpass in the loop) is exactly "echo with a filter in the feedback path". — [JOS: Lowpass Feedback Comb Filter](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Lowpass_Feedback_Comb_Filter.html); [K] [JOS: Delay-Line Interpolation](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Delay_Line_Interpolation.html)
- Dattorro's "Effect Design" Part 1 (JAES 45(9), 1997, pp. 660–684) covers the plate reverberator and allpass/other filters; [K] Part 2 covers delay-line modulation and chorus (interpolated modulated delays). — [Dattorro Part 1 PDF (CCRMA)](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~dattorro/EffectDesignPart1.pdf); [K] [Dattorro Part 2 PDF](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~dattorro/EffectDesignPart2.pdf)
- Variable-delay practice: either write at a fixed rate and vary the read rate (tape/BBD behaviour, produces pitch glide) or handle delay-time jumps another way; "handling continuously variable delay times is a lot more difficult". — [Cycling '74 gen~ tape delay thread](https://cycling74.com/forums/gen~-tape-delay-emulation-tape-speed)
- [K] Web Audio constraint: a native `DelayNode` inside a feedback cycle is clamped to at least one render quantum (128 frames, about 2.9 ms at 44.1 kHz), and native-node graphs cannot put arbitrary per-sample nonlinearities inside the loop cheaply — a reason to implement the whole echo inside one AudioWorkletProcessor. — [W3C Web Audio API spec, DelayNode](https://www.w3.org/TR/webaudio/#DelayNode)

#### Resonant LPF / HPF / BPF sweeps
- RBJ "Audio EQ Cookbook" biquad formulas (LPF, HPF, BPF, notch, peaking, shelves) are the standard coefficient reference; now published as a W3C Note. — [K] [W3C Audio EQ Cookbook](https://www.w3.org/TR/audio-eq-cookbook/)
- For fast knob sweeps a zero-delay-feedback / trapezoidal state-variable filter is preferred: Andrew Simper's (Cytomic) SVF papers describe a trapezoidal-integration SVF "suited to audio rate modulation of filter coefficients", giving LP/BP/HP/notch/peak simultaneously; 12 dB/oct, Q = 0.707 is non-peaking and very high Q self-oscillates. — [Cytomic technical papers index](https://cytomic.com/technical-papers/); [K] [SvfLinearTrapOptimised2.pdf](https://cytomic.com/files/dsp/SvfLinearTrapOptimised2.pdf); C++ port with those notes: [hollance gist](https://gist.github.com/hollance/2891d89c57adc71d9560bcf0e1e55c4b); docs of an independent port: [OpenWare AbstractStateVariableFilter](https://www.rebeltech.org/docs/classAbstractStateVariableFilter.html)
- The older Chamberlin digital SVF (cheap, but unstable at high cutoff without oversampling) is explained at EarLevel. — [EarLevel: The digital state variable filter](https://www.earlevel.com/main/2003/03/02/the-digital-state-variable-filter/)
- [K] Theory reference for TPT/ZDF filters: Vadim Zavalishin, *The Art of VA Filter Design* (free PDF). — [Native Instruments PDF](https://www.native-instruments.com/fileadmin/ni_media/downloads/pdf/VAFilterDesign_2.1.0.pdf)

#### 3-band isolator / kill EQ
- Linkwitz-Riley filters are made by cascading two Butterworth filters (LR4 = two 2nd-order Butterworths); low + high outputs sum to an allpass (flat magnitude, shifted phase). — [Wikipedia: Linkwitz–Riley filter](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Linkwitz-Riley_filter); [Linkwitz Lab: crossovers](https://linkwitzlab.com/crossovers.htm)
- Multi-band pitfall: with more than two bands the naive sum is no longer allpass; "lpX ± hpX = apX", so a 3-band LR bank needs one compensating allpass (at the upper crossover frequency) in the low band. — [KVR thread on multiband LR allpass compensation](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=3985874); [Cycling '74 3-band LR thread](https://cycling74.com/forums/3-band-linkwitz-riley-crossover-filter-design)
- Alternative: derive the mid band by subtracting low and high from the input (perfect reconstruction at unity gains, at the cost of poorer mid-band slopes). — [JUCE forum "Perfect crossover filters"](https://forum.juce.com/t/perfect-crossover-filters/36125)
- Mixxx ships several open EQ/isolator designs to study (not copy): Biquad Equalizer, **Biquad Full Kill Equalizer**, Bessel and Linkwitz-Riley isolator variants, parametric EQ. — [Mixxx manual: Effects](https://manual.mixxx.org/2.3/en/chapters/effects.html); [Mixxx manual appendix (version history of added effects)](https://manual.mixxx.org/2.1/en/chapters/appendix)

#### Algorithmic reverb (Reverb Up / Reverb Down, "Space")
- Freeverb: public-domain C++ by "Jezar at Dreampoint"; four series Schroeder diffusion allpasses and eight parallel Schroeder-Moorer lowpass-feedback comb filters per channel; its "allpass" is really a feedback comb in series with a feedforward comb (an approximation). — [JOS PASP: Freeverb](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Freeverb.html); [JOS: Freeverb allpass approximation](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/lumped/Freeverb_Allpass_Approximation.html)
- Schroeder reverberators (JCRev: three allpasses + four combs, tuned by John Chowning) are documented by JOS and implemented as `JCReverb` in Tone.js. — [JOS: Example Schroeder Reverberators](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Example_Schroeder_Reverberators.html); [Tone.js JCReverb docs](https://tonejs.github.io/docs/r12/JCReverb)
- Dattorro plate: figure-of-eight "tank" of modulated allpasses and delays with input diffusers; the paper notes the structure is best explored by tinkering. A Faust implementation that cites the paper exists (`re.dattorro_rev`). — [Dattorro Part 1 PDF](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~dattorro/EffectDesignPart1.pdf); [Faust reverbs library](https://faustlibraries.grame.fr/libs/reverbs/)
- FDN: N delay lines mixed by a lossless (unitary) feedback matrix; Zita-Rev1 is an FDN plus Schroeder allpass combs. — [JOS PASP: Zita-Rev1](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Zita_Rev1.html); [K] [JOS: Feedback Delay Networks](https://ccrma.stanford.edu/~jos/pasp/Feedback_Delay_Networks_FDN.html); theory: [Schlecht & Habets, "On Lossless Feedback Delay Networks" (arXiv)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.07729)
- Signalsmith's "Let's Write A Reverb" (Geraint Luff) is a widely recommended practical FDN tutorial: multichannel diffusion steps followed by an FDN with a Householder mixing matrix; Hadamard mixing is denser but still needs a few loops to reach good echo density, hence separate diffusers. — secondary descriptions: [Stretch Armstrong blog](https://meijisirlnd.github.io/releases/2023/05/22/MatrixReverb.html), [KVR FDN thread with Signalsmith's comments](https://www.kvraudio.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=8122342); [K] original: [signalsmith-audio.co.uk/writing/2021/lets-write-a-reverb](https://signalsmith-audio.co.uk/writing/2021/lets-write-a-reverb/)
- Recent research: DAFx-26 paper on parametric lossless FDN matrices with an in-place O(N log N) feedback operation (Fast Walsh-Hadamard-like). — [DAFx26 paper 04](https://www.dafx.de/paper-archive/2026/papers/DAFx26_paper_04.pdf)

#### Spiral (pitch-shifting feedback delay, Shepard-style rise/fall)
- The "shimmer" recipe is the public template: "create a feedback loop, incorporating a pitch shifter set to +1 octave, and a reverb with a fairly long decay"; common elements are a pitch shifter, a modulated reverb/delay, feedback and EQ. — [Valhalla DSP: Eno/Lanois shimmer sound, how it is made](https://valhalladsp.com/2010/05/11/enolanois-shimmer-sound-how-it-is-made/)
- Practical notes from Valhalla: a splice-optimising ("deglitched") shifter cannot find good splice points inside a reverb loop because the phase is continually scrambled; a simple early-style delay-line shifter is adequate; rolling off highs in the loop removes most aliasing in the pitch shifter's feedback path. — [Valhalla: Shimmer, modulation, auto-correlation and decorrelation](https://valhalladsp.com/?p=221); [Valhalla: ValhallaShimmer, a bit of history](https://valhalladsp.com/?p=549); [Valhalla: tips and tricks, shimmering](https://valhalladsp.com/2010/12/02/valhallashimmer-tips-and-tricks-shimmering/)
- Tone.js `PitchShift` is a real-time delay-line pitch shifter whose "window size corresponds roughly to the sample length in a looping sampler" (two crossfaded, sawtooth-modulated delay taps). — [Tone.js PitchShift docs](https://tonejs.github.io/docs/r11/PitchShift)
- Higher-quality option: Signalsmith Stretch, a polyphonic pitch/time library with an official WASM/AudioWorklet web build. — [signalsmith-stretch on npm](https://npmjs.com/package/signalsmith-stretch)

#### Bitcrusher / sample-rate reduction (Crush Echo)
- Tone.js `BitCrusher` takes a bit depth and produces digital distortion; Mixxx's Bit Crusher has bit depth and downsampling controls. — [Tone.js effects overview](https://tessl.io/registry/tessl/npm-tone/files/docs/effects.md); [Mixxx manual: Effects](https://manual.mixxx.org/2.3/en/chapters/effects.html)
- [K] musicdsp.org's classic "Decimator" snippet combines bit reduction (`floor(x * 2^bits) / 2^bits`) with sample-and-hold rate reduction driven by a phase accumulator. — [K] [musicdsp: Decimator](https://www.musicdsp.org/en/latest/Effects/124-decimator.html)

#### Noise sweep (Noise)
- Manual-described structure is itself a recipe: white noise -> band-pass filter (knob = cutoff) -> echo, with a level control and a modulation control. — [manualsdir p.15](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=15)
- Pink noise: Paul Kellett's filter (sum of several one-pole lowpasses applied to white noise, about −3 dB/oct) on musicdsp; coefficients are sample-rate dependent (accuracy degrades if 44.1 kHz coefficients are reused at higher rates). Rust `fundsp` documents Kellett's method as ±0.05 dB above 9.2 Hz at 44.1 kHz. — [musicdsp: Pink noise filter](https://www.musicdsp.org/en/latest/Filters/76-pink-noise-filter.html); [fundsp filter.rs](https://docs.rs/crate/fundsp/0.23.0/source/src/filter.rs)

#### Beat roll / loop slicer / stutter, and Trans (beat-synced gate)
- Manual-level algorithm for roll: start recording when the control is engaged, then repeat the captured slice at the selected beat multiple; X-Pad ROLL samples "the sound at the time the pad is touched" with length set by position. — [manualslib p.18](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/681089/Pioneer-Remix-Station-Rmx-1000.html?page=18); [manualslib.es p.13](https://manualslib.es/manual/7919/Pioneer-Rmx-1000.html?page=13)
- Trans: "cuts the input sound according to the multiplying factor of the beat" — a beat-synced amplitude gate (square/trapezoid tremolo). Mixxx ships Tremolo and Autopan effects with beat-synced rate as open reference designs. — [manualsdir p.17](https://www.manualsdir.com/manuals/486033/pioneer-rmx-1000-m.html?page=17); [Mixxx manual appendix](https://manual.mixxx.org/2.1/en/chapters/appendix)

#### Vinyl brake and back spin (variable-rate playback of buffered audio)
- Generic method: keep writing the live signal to a buffer and read it with a variable-rate (interpolated) read head; a buffer-based stop can never read "past now", so speed-ups require prior buffering. — [Gearspace thread on buffer-based tape stops](https://gearspace.com/threads/cableguys-timestop-instant-creative-tape-stops.1469696/); [Cycling '74 gen~ thread](https://cycling74.com/forums/gen~-tape-delay-emulation-tape-speed)
- Example parameterisation from an open DJ engine PR: ramp the platter rate linearly to zero over a configurable stop time (default 0.5 s, range 0.05–3.0 s). — [geovannimp/mixar PR #533](https://github.com/geovannimp/mixar/pull/533) (repo license not checked)
- Mixxx exposes `engine.brake(deck, activate, factor, rate)`, `engine.spinback(...)` and `engine.softStart(...)` to controller scripts; developers discuss an exponential-style approach to the target rate (slow final phase) versus a linear ramp `rate(t) = accel * (t - t0)`, and note that spinback can sound unnatural with poor parameters. — [Mixxx bug 1090780](https://bugs.launchpad.net/mixxx/+bug/1090780); [Mixxx bug 1812483](https://bugs.launchpad.net/bugs/1812483); [Mixxx bug 1967715](https://bugs.launchpad.net/bugs/1967715); [proposal to make brake/spinback effects, bug 1692261](https://bugs.launchpad.net/mixxx/+bug/1692261)

#### Reverse delay ("Rev Delay", RMX-500/IGNITE "Reverse")
- No authoritative tutorial was surfaced in this session; see Inferences for the standard construction and Gaps.

### Inferences
(Engineering guidance synthesised from the sources above plus standard practice; not individually cited.)
- **One worklet, shared primitives.** Implement as a single `AudioWorkletProcessor` (or one per section) owning: `DelayLine` (power-of-two ring buffer, linear or 4-point Hermite read), `Svf` (TPT), `OnePole`, `Biquad` (RBJ), `Allpass`, `NoiseGen`, `EnvelopeFollower`, `Smoother` (one-pole parameter slew at about 5–20 ms), `BeatClock` (BPM + phase). Avoid allocations in `process()`; pre-allocate about 4–8 s of ring buffer per channel for roll/brake/backspin/reverse.
- **Echo core.** `y = dry*x + wet*d; write = x*send + sat(filter(d))*fb`. Delay samples = `sr * 60/BPM * ratio`. Put the SVF (BP/HP/LP per effect type) and a soft clipper (`tanh` or cubic) *inside* the loop so feedback near or above 1.0 stays bounded; a DC blocker in the loop is prudent. For "Break Down" macros, tie knob -> filter cutoff sweep + rising send + falling dry.
- **Changing delay time.** Two behaviours: (a) slew the read position (tape-style pitch glide; desirable for Spiral and manual sweeps), (b) dual-tap crossfade (about 20–50 ms) between old and new delay time (click-free, no pitch artefact; desirable when BPM updates or ratio buttons change).
- **Spiral.** Feedback delay with a delay-line pitch shifter in the loop: two read taps whose delays ramp with sawtooth phase 180° apart, each windowed (Hann/triangular) and summed; ratio > 1 for "up", < 1 for "down"; each trip around the loop compounds the shift, giving the endless rising/falling (Shepard-like) spiral. Lowpass in the loop to hide aliasing/splice artefacts (per Valhalla). Add knob-driven delay-time slew for the simultaneous pitch bend the manual describes.
- **Reverb Up/Down.** Freeverb is cheapest (8 combs + 4 allpasses per channel; scale the published 44.1 kHz delay lengths by `sr/44100`); Dattorro plate or an 8-line Householder/Hadamard FDN with input diffusers sounds denser. Macro: knob -> wet level + decay; "Up" variant can add a rising HPF on the wet/dry sum, "Down" a falling LPF — these filter pairings are a design guess, not manual facts.
- **Isolator.** LR4 split at two crossovers (typical DJ values are in the region of 200–350 Hz and 2–4 kHz; Pioneer's actual values are unpublished): `low = LP1(x)`, `rest = HP1(x)`, `mid = LP2(rest)`, `high = HP2(rest)`, and `low` passed through an allpass equal to `LP2+HP2` (i.e. a 2nd-order allpass at f2) so the three bands sum flat. Per-band gain from 0 (kill) to about +6…+9 dB with smoothed gains. The other Isolate modes are per-band inserts before the sum: CUT (band filter sweep) / ADD (band-limited echo send), TRANS (beat gate) / ROLL (band-limited loop capture), GATE (envelope-follower noise gate with threshold from the knob) / DRIVE (pre-gain into `tanh` waveshaper with 2× oversampling or ADAA to limit aliasing, plus make-up gain).
- **Crush.** `hold` counter for sample-rate reduction (`if (phase += rate/sr) >= 1 then latch`), then quantise `round(x * q) / q` with `q = 2^(bits-1)`; follow with a LPF and the echo. Morph the knob over rate first, then bits, for a musical range.
- **Noise.** `Math.random()*2-1` (or a cheap xorshift PRNG for determinism) -> SVF band-pass with knob-mapped cutoff (exponential, e.g. 200 Hz -> 12 kHz) -> echo; SP1 = level; SP2 = LFO depth on cutoff or amplitude (beat-synced).
- **Roll / stutter.** Always record input into a ring buffer. On trigger (optionally quantised), mark `start = writePos - 0` and capture `len = beats * T * sr`; after the first pass, loop the slice with a 3–10 ms crossfade at the loop seam; while held, changing the division shortens/lengthens the loop from the same start. A "slip" design keeps the live input muted but running so release returns to the correct song position (inherent for a pass-through effect).
- **Trans.** Gain = pulse wave locked to beat phase (duty about 50 %), with 2–5 ms attack/release ramps to avoid clicks; rate from beat ratio.
- **Vinyl brake.** On trigger, freeze "live" and read the ring buffer with `rate(t)` falling from 1 to 0 over the chosen time (linear, or `rate = (1 - t/T)^k` with `k` about 1.5–2 for a more platter-like curve); since `rate <= 1` the read head only falls behind the write head, so no look-ahead is needed. Fade output to zero as rate approaches 0; on lever release, crossfade back to live input. Use Hermite/cubic interpolation; pitch drops naturally.
- **Back spin.** Read head runs *backwards* from the trigger point: `rate(t)` starts strongly negative (e.g. −2…−4×) and decays toward 0 over about 0.5–1 beat/bar, then cut or crossfade to live. Needs only as much recorded history as the integral of |rate|. Interpolated reads at |rate| > 1 alias; a simple fix is a LPF tracking 1/|rate| or 2× oversampled reads.
- **Release echo.** Mute the echo's input (and the dry path), let feedback run at a beat-synced time (commonly 1/2 or 3/4 beat) with fb about 0.6–0.8 and a darkening LPF in the loop; on release, reset all other effect states and restore dry.
- **Reverse delay.** Record into a buffer; every segment of length L (a beat fraction) play the previous segment backwards (read pointer decrements while write increments), with a short fade window at each segment boundary; use two overlapping read heads offset by L/2 with complementary windows to avoid gaps. Optional feedback into the writer gives repeating reversed echoes. Latency is inherently one segment.
- **"Offset"/beat-masher (RMX-500).** Repeat or re-order buffered beat slices with a phase offset relative to the beat grid (same ring buffer as roll, slice index remapped).
- **Sampler.** One-shot voices from decoded `AudioBuffer` data copied into the worklet (or `AudioBufferSourceNode`s scheduled on the beat grid from the main thread); pad position selects retrigger subdivision; pitch = playback-rate; "overdub" = a 4-beat step/event list recorded against beat phase.

## 4. Real-time BPM detection algorithms suitable for a browser, and JS/WASM implementations

### Takeaway
The classic pipeline is onset-strength envelope -> periodicity analysis (comb-filter bank or autocorrelation) -> tempo pick with an octave prior -> beat-phase tracking; Scheirer (1998), Ellis (2007) and Percival & Tzanetakis (2014) are the canonical references. In JavaScript, `realtime-bpm-analyzer` (AudioWorklet, Apache-2.0 per its README) and `web-audio-beat-detector` (MIT) are permissive; essentia.js (AGPL-3.0) and aubio-based ports (GPL-3) are copyleft and should be reference-only for an MIT project.

### Cited Findings
- Scheirer, "Tempo and beat analysis of acoustic musical signals", JASA 103(1), 1998, pp. 588–601: a small number of band-pass filters followed by banks of parallel comb filters (a resonator per candidate period T with decay α) to find tempo and beat phase in real time, causally. — [paper PDF (Columbia mirror)](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/papers/Schei98-beats.pdf); [Columbia E4896 lecture slides](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/e4896/lectures/E4896-L10.pdf)
- Ellis, "Beat Tracking by Dynamic Programming", J. New Music Research 36(1), 2007, pp. 51–60: onset-strength envelope -> global tempo estimate (autocorrelation with a perceptual weighting) -> dynamic programming to choose beat times maximising onset strength and regular spacing; the DP searches all beat sequences in linear time. Offline/global by design. — [paper PDF](https://hajim.rochester.edu/ece/sites/zduan/teaching/ece472/reading/Ellis_2007.pdf); [LabROSA beat-tracking page](https://www.ee.columbia.edu/~dpwe/LabROSA/projects/beattrack/)
- Percival & Tzanetakis, "Streamlined Tempo Estimation Based on Autocorrelation and Cross-correlation With Pulses", IEEE/ACM TASLP 22(12), 2014, pp. 1765–1776: autocorrelation of the onset-strength signal for self-similarity, then cross-correlation with regularly spaced pulse trains to score candidates; designed to minimise steps and parameters. — [DBLP record](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/journals/taslp/PercivalT14.html)
- OBTAIN (2017): a real-time beat-tracking system for audio signals, a further reference for causal operation. — [arXiv 1704.02216](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1704.02216)
- **realtime-bpm-analyzer** (dlepaux): TypeScript, zero dependencies, AudioWorklet-based (`createRealTimeBpmProcessor(audioContext)` returns an AudioWorkletNode; newer docs show `createRealtimeBpmAnalyzer`), works on files, audio nodes, streams and microphone; lineage is Tornqvist's bpm-detective and Joe Sullivan's peak/interval method. **License conflict**: README says Apache-2.0 (with a note to contact the author for commercial licensing) while the npm sidebar/Yarn metadata show MIT. — [npm page](https://npmjs.com/package/realtime-bpm-analyzer); [project docs](https://dlepaux.github.io/realtime-bpm-analyzer/); [depscope mirror](https://depscope.dev/pkg/npm/realtime-bpm-analyzer)
- **web-audio-beat-detector** (chrisguttandin): MIT [V]; npm 8.2.x; a Web Audio beat/tempo detection utility. — [npm page](https://www.npmjs.com/package/web-audio-beat-detector); [jsDelivr package page (MIT)](https://www.jsdelivr.com/package/npm/web-audio-beat-detector)
- **essentia.js** (MTG/UPF): AGPL-3.0 [V] (C++ Essentia backend is also AGPLv3); WASM build of Essentia's MIR algorithms; npm 0.1.3 last published about five years ago. — [GitHub](https://github.com/MTG/essentia.js); [npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/essentia.js); [TISMIR article](https://transactions.ismir.net/articles/10.5334/tismir.111)
- **aubio** (C library): GPL-3.0-or-later [V]; **aubiojs** (WASM wrapper, npm `aubiojs`) — license not confirmed by search. — [aubio repo mirror](https://github.com/ensonic/aubio); [aubiojs on npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/aubiojs)
- **bpm-detective** (Tornqvist) and **bpm.js**: small offline detectors in the Joe Sullivan lineage. — [bpm-detective on npm](https://npmjs.com/package/bpm-detective); [bpm.js](https://github.com/juliangruber/bpm.js) (licenses not checked)
- Hardware benchmark: the RMX-1000's auto BPM covers 70–180 BPM and is described by a reviewer as good but slow to follow large tempo changes. — [Pioneer DJ specs](https://www.pioneerdj.com/en-gb/product/effector/archive/rmx-1000/black/specifications/); [DJWorx review](https://djworx.com/review-pioneer-rmx-1000-remix-station/)

### Inferences
- A from-scratch AudioWorklet detector (no third-party code) is modest: (1) onset strength = half-wave-rectified spectral flux (512–1024-sample FFT, hop about 5–10 ms) or, cheaper, low-passed/differentiated energy envelopes in 3–6 bands as in Scheirer; (2) keep 6–8 s of envelope; (3) every about 0.5 s compute autocorrelation (or a comb-filter bank) over lags for 70–180 BPM; (4) score with a log-Gaussian prior around about 120–128 BPM and check half/double candidates (Percival-style pulse-train cross-correlation resolves octave errors and also yields phase); (5) smooth with hysteresis so the display does not jitter; (6) beat phase = argmax of cross-correlation of the recent envelope with a pulse train at the chosen period; then a PLL to track drift.
- For DJ material (steady four-on-the-floor) the peak-interval-histogram method used by realtime-bpm-analyzer is often sufficient and cheap, but gives BPM only, not reliable beat phase; quantised effects need phase, so plan on the pulse-train/comb approach or on getting beat grids from the host app.
- If the app already has track beat grids (as a DJ app usually does), use them and treat audio BPM detection as a fallback for external/line input, mirroring the RMX's AUTO -> TAP fallback.
- AGPL (essentia.js) is the most restrictive option for a web app: network use triggers source-distribution obligations, so it is unsuitable as a dependency of an MIT project; GPL-3 aubio ports likewise.

### Gaps
- realtime-bpm-analyzer's actual LICENSE file (named `licence.md` per the README) could not be opened; the Apache-2.0 vs MIT discrepancy and the "commercial licensing" note must be resolved by reading the repo before depending on it.
- aubiojs, bpm-detective and bpm.js licenses unverified. [K] BTrack (Adam Stark's real-time C++ beat tracker, GPL-3) and Mixxx's use of the Queen Mary qm-dsp library (GPL) are relevant references but were not verified this session.
- No benchmark comparing JS BPM detectors' accuracy/latency on DJ material was found.
- Whether web-audio-beat-detector supports streaming input (it is believed to analyse whole AudioBuffers offline) was not confirmed.

### Inferences
- Safe-to-port tier (verify LICENSE files first): Freeverb (PD), Tone.js (MIT), Mutable Instruments STM32 code (MIT), signalsmith-stretch (MIT), web-audio-beat-detector (MIT). Keep the upstream copyright/permission notices in ported files and in a THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES file.
- Conditional tier: Faust library functions (per-function check), realtime-bpm-analyzer (license ambiguity), LGPL libraries such as SoundTouch/SoundTouchJS [K] (LGPL allows linking but ported/derived source stays LGPL, which is awkward in a bundled JS app).
- Reference-only tier: Mixxx, Rubber Band, aubio/aubiojs, Essentia/essentia.js, and [K] BTrack, qm-dsp, Surge XT, Dragonfly Reverb (all GPL/AGPL). Reading them for understanding is fine; line-by-line translation into the MIT codebase creates a derivative work. Prefer implementing from the cited papers/tutorials instead.
- Algorithms and formulas published in papers and tutorials (RBJ cookbook, Dattorro's plate, JOS's books, Cytomic's SVF equations, Scheirer/Ellis/Percival) are not themselves code under a software license; re-implementing them from the descriptions is the cleanest route for an original engine. (Engineering judgement, not legal advice.)
- [K] Other permissive DSP codebases worth checking as porting sources: DaisySP (MIT; Electro-Smith), Airwindows (MIT), Signalsmith DSP library (MIT). None were verified in this session.
- Branding: effect *types* (echo, roll, gate, brake, backspin, isolator) are generic, but "RMX", "Remix Station", "Scene FX", "X-Pad" and "remixbox" are Pioneer DJ/AlphaTheta product terms; an original engine should use its own names and its own parameter mappings/presets.
